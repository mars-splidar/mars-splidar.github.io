MaRS: Markov-Renewal Single-Photon LiDAR Simulator

MaRS: Markov-Renewal Single-Photon LiDAR Simulator

Accurate and efficient single-photon LiDAR simulation under detector dead time.

Weijian Zhang · Prateek Chennuri · Hashan K. Weerasooriya · Bole Ma · Stanley H. Chan

Elmore Family School of ECE, Purdue University

    Cube slicer

    Look inside the three cubes

    Click a pixel to see when its photons were registered. Dead time shifts the peak earlier. While Poisson ignores this, the gold standard and MaRS show it.

    In short

    A single-photon detector goes blind for a short dead time after every detection, and that breaks the statistics simulators rely on. Fast Poisson models ignore it. The sequential gold standard handles it correctly but is too slow to generate training data. MaRS derives the first analytic photon-count law under dead time, matching the gold standard’s statistics about 40× faster on a 128×128×1024 cube.

    Results

    Accuracy

    The first analytic photon-count distribution under dead time.

    Against the sequential gold standard, renewal theory misplaces the count and the best prior model misjudges its spread. MaRS matches both, with a grid-averaged KL 30× below the best prior model’s.

    Efficiency

    Fast enough for training data

    The gold standard checks every photon, pixel by pixel. Because our count model is analytic, MaRS computes all the statistics once and stores them in a lookup table. Simulation then fills every pixel and realization in one step.

    Downstream

    Ignoring dead time biases downstream networks

    We trained the same network on each simulator’s data and tested it on gold-standard data. Trained on Poisson, it fails. Trained on MaRS, it works.

    Ablation

    Why an accurate count model matters

    We keep everything else fixed and change only the photon-count model. The closer it is to the truth, the better the network does on gold-standard data.
